# Slide development notes

Sources and design decisions for the decks in this directory.

## [lect05d-v2-probability_concepts.djot](lect05d-v2-probability_concepts.djot)

- **Slide 2 - Learning Objectives.** State the tasks in the same order as the lecture.
- **Slide 3 - Probability speak.** Provide a reference for notation before the examples.
- **Slide 6 - What makes this cross difficult?** Give the opening problem a plan, rather than leaving it behind without explanation.
- **Slide 7 - Addition Rule.** Mark the first tool: the probability that either event occurs.
- **Slide 8 - One gamete, one allele at this locus.** Ground mutually exclusive in an ordinary haploid gamete before stating the rule.
- **Slide 9 - Rule card: addition.** Attach the formula to the gamete example just seen.
- **Slide 10 - Now ask about one offspring.** Restore the original full question and parent-to-gamete visual explanation.
- **Slide 11 - Does every offspring qualify?** Make the incorrect shortcut a claim to investigate.
- **Slide 12 - Test the claim with a Punnett square.** Provide a large shared work surface, as in the original deck.
- **Slide 13 - Three of the four offspring types qualify.** Answer the vote from the visible offspring, before repairing the equation.
- **Slide 14 - Adding counted the shared cell twice.** Explain the shortcut's error in the same square and positions.
- **Slide 15 - Rule card: general addition.** Generalize the correction students just explained.
- **Slide 16 - Multiplication Rule.** Introduce multiplication as the calculation for the shared cell, not a new story.
- **Slide 17 - Calculate the probability of that shared cell.** Explain multiplication as a fraction of a fraction in the familiar square.
- **Slide 18 - Rule card: multiplication.** Attach the independent-events formula to the preceding calculation.
- **Slide 19 - Independent is not mutually exclusive.** Distinguish the two conditions using examples already taught.
- **Slide 20 - Applying both rules.** Return to the opening question and fulfill the promise made there.
- **Slide 21 - &#127804; Which pollen can fertilize?** Separate the biological change from the probability rules.
- **Slide 22 - Count only pollen that can fertilize.** Answer the vote and put the complete inputs together.
- **Slide 23 - Multiply to fill the F&#x2082; square.** Apply multiplication, with one worked cell and three student calculations.
- **Slide 24 - The four cells are no longer equally likely.** Finish multiplication and motivate addition without changing examples.
- **Slide 25 - _Aa_: multiply within, add between.** Connect the rules within one calculation.
- **Slide 26 - &#127793; Our F&#x2082; prediction, solved.** Resolve the opening challenge: AA:Aa:aa = 1:3:2.
- **Slide 27 - Why did the familiar 1:2:1 ratio change?** Make the solution's lesson explicit and give the main narrative a clear ending.
- **Slide 28 - Conditional probability.** Introduce a new question after resolving the opening cross.
- **Slide 29 - Follow an allele through a family.** State the full family problem with enough context for independent study.
- **Slide 30 - The child's probability depends on the son.** Answer the son's question and make the child's question depend visibly on it.
- **Slide 31 - Follow _H_ from son to child.** Derive one-quarter by following the route that transmits H through both generations.
- **Slide 32 - What changes when the son is _Hh_?** Give conditional probability meaning before introducing the vertical bar.
- **Slide 33 - General Multiplication Rule.** Name the rule already used along the branch; it is not an extra recipe.
- **Slide 34 - Exit check: name the condition.** Check the three lessons using only familiar examples.
- **Slide 37 - Addition Rules.** Single-slide homework reference, repeating the earlier addition rule cards in one place.
- **Slide 38 - Multiplication Rules.** Single-slide homework reference, repeating the earlier multiplication rule cards in one place.
- **Slide 39 - Study reference: choosing a rule.** These paragraphs make the rule conditions available in the exported slides, without requiring access to notes.
- **Slide 41 - Study reference: the family example.** Provide a complete offline explanation.
- **Slide 42 - Study reference: reasoning backward.** Preserve the reverse-condition and Bayes explanation without extending the live narrative. Define the extra term where it appears in this optional reference.
- **Slide 43 - Summary: context before calculation.** Close with the sequence that solved the opening problem.

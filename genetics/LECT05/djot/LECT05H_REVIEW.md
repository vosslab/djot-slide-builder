## Content verification

- Independently recomputed all 16 genotype entries for each pea, squash, and Labrador square.
  The full squares are symmetric and use the same four gametes for eggs and pollen/sperm.
- Checked phenotype totals: pea 9:7, squash 12:3:1, and Labrador 9:3:4. All totals equal 16.
- Independently summed the nine overview color groups; ratios match the source figure, including
  unreduced 12:4 and 10:6. Tested all four-outcome testcross groupings against the F2 groupings.
- Independently assorting loci and complete dominance within each locus are explicit assumptions
  on the example summary. Epistasis groups phenotypes; it does not change Mendelian segregation.
- The cat ratio alone cannot identify the biological gene names. The source table highlight used
  aaB_ while its answer used A_bb. The revision uses A_bb consistently to agree with answer d,
  without treating the letter names as experimentally determined loci.
- The two-step C/P pathway remains a teaching model. A functional downstream P step cannot make
  pigment if cc prevents production of the intermediate. The blocked-step sequence now states this.

The [Nature Education discussion](https://www.nature.com/scitable/topicpage/epistasis-gene-interaction-and-phenotype-effects-460/)
corroborates the supplied C/P teaching model and flower counts. The cited
[Dooner, Robbins, and Jorgensen paper](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.ge.25.120191.001133)
is a 1991 review of anthocyanin biosynthesis. Its bibliographic record does not substantiate the
specific claim that these two genes were discovered exactly 82 years after the historical cross.
The revision retains the biochemistry reference and replaces that discovery-date claim with the
model description. This is a bounded bibliographic check, not a full historical literature review.

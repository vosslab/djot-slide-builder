#6 Plasmid Vectors – Sowmya Reddy
1.Plasmid vectors ensure that a DNA fragment has been successfully inserted through the use of selectable markers, such as antibiotic resistance genes. Here's how this works:
Selectable Markers: Plasmid vectors often contain genes that provide resistance to antibiotics
Insertion of DNA Fragment: When a researcher inserts a DNA fragment into the plasmid vector, it disrupts or replaces a part of the plasmid DNA sequence. 
Transforming Host Cells: The plasmid vector, with the inserted DNA fragment, is then introduced into host cells.
Selection: After transformation, the host cells are grown on a medium containing the antibiotic corresponding to the resistance gene present in the plasmid
Identification: Colonies of antibiotic-resistant cells will grow on the selection medium. Each of these colonies is expected to contain cells with the plasmid vector carrying the DNA fragment of interest.

#8 in vitro DNA synthesis – Freny
In vitro: 
conducted outside of a living organism, typically in a controlled laboratory environment.
In in vitro DNA synthesis, enzymes like DNA polymerase are used to replicate DNA outside of a living cell.

In vivo: 
conducted within a living organism, whether it's a whole organism (e.g., a mouse or a human) or within specific tissues or organs of the organism. 
In in vivo DNA replication occurs within living cells, where a complex set of enzymes, including DNA helicase, primase, and DNA polymerase.

Benefits of in vitro DNA synthesis:
Precision: Allows controlled manipulation and customization of DNA sequences.
Versatility: Useful for applications like PCR, DNA sequencing, and gene cloning.
Speed: Faster results, not reliant on cell growth.

Limitations:
Lack of cellular context: Misses the intricacies of in vivo regulation and function.
Limited scale: Typically produces shorter DNA fragments.
Cost: Can be expensive due to reagent and equipment requirements.

Pyrosequencing technique : Its method is called pyrosequencing or sequencing by synthesis. It works like this.
·        The DNA to be sequenced is broken up into fragments of ~100 base pairs and denatured to form single-stranded DNA (ssDNA).
·        Single ssDNA fragments are attached to microscopic beads, which are separated from each other.
·        Pyrosequencing has  heretofore been used for generating short sequence reads (1-100 nucleotides).
Application : Pyrosequencing can be used to detect DNA and RNA from almost any source, and has applications in bacteria resistance screening, drug development, epigenetics, and many other fields.
Sequencing by synthesis :Sequencing by synthesis is a DNA sequencing method in which DNA polymerases and dNTPs are used to replicate (synthesize) the strand to be sequenced
Ion Semiconductor Sequencing :Ion semiconductor sequencing is a method of DNA sequencing based on the detection of hydrogen ions that are released during the polymerization of DNA.

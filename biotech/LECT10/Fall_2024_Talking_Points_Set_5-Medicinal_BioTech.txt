#6 Liposomes in Gene Delivery - Sai
…..
…..
Structure: Liposomes – spherical vesicles of cholesterol and phospholipids – encapsulate genes in a protective lipid bilayer for targeted delivery.
Production: Created via sonication/extrusion; consistent sizing is challenging due to lipid variability and aggregation.
Immune Evasion: Mimic cell membranes to evade immune detection; pegylation adds stealth by reducing immune recognition.
pH-Sensitivity: Destabilize in acidic environments (e.g., endosomes) to release genetic payload directly into the cytoplasm.
Viral Comparison: Safer than viral vectors with lower immunogenicity, but have lower efficiency and limited payload capacity.
Limitations: Limited to small genes; may cause inflammation, hypersensitivity, and rapid bloodstream clearance.

#6 Liposomes in Gene Delivery - Dipali
Definition: Liposomes are tiny, spherical vesicles made of lipid bilayers, similar to cell membranes.
Role in Gene Delivery:
Encapsulate and protect genetic material (DNA, RNA) during transport.
Improve stability and increase efficiency of gene delivery to cells.

How Liposomes Are Made:

Dissolve lipids, dry into film, then rehydrate to form vesicles.

Control size with sonication or extrusion methods.


Challenges in Consistent Size:

Variability in lipid types and production methods.

Hard to scale up from lab to industrial production.

Liposomes often vary in size, affecting delivery efficiency.

#7 Gene Therapy in Cancer - Vishal
There have been several trials and treatments for cancer that have shown promising results, particularly in the area of immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and gene therapy. Some notable advancements include:
1. CAR-T Cell Therapy: This is a form of immunotherapy where a patient's T cells are modified to better attack cancer cells. It has shown significant success in treating certain types of blood cancers, such as acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and some lymphomas.

2. Oncolytic Virus Therapy: This involves using genetically modified viruses that selectively infect and kill cancer cells. One example is the FDA-approved therapy talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC) for melanoma.

3.CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing: While still largely in experimental stages, studies are investigating its use to modify genes in cancer cells to make them more susceptible to treatment.

4.Gene Therapy Approvals: The FDA has approved a few gene therapies related to cancer, such as:
   - Kymriah (tisagenlecleucel): A CAR-T cell therapy for certain leukemias and lymphomas.
   - Yescarta (axicabtagene ciloleucel): Another CAR-T cell therapy for large B-cell lymphoma.

Regarding nanoparticles in cancer gene therapy. Nanoparticles can serve as delivery vehicles for gene therapies, allowing for targeted delivery to cancer cells while minimizing damage to healthy tissues. This approach enhances the efficacy of gene therapies and reduces potential side effects, making it a promising area of research.

#11 CRISPR Native Bacteria Function - Yamini
Discovery: CRISPR was first observed by Francisco Mojica (1990s) in bacteria as repetitive DNA sequences with viral "spacers," suggesting an immune function.
CRISPR Acronym: Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats.
Purpose: Bacterial immune system that stores viral DNA (spacers) to recognize and fight future infections.
.
CRISPR System: Found in many bacteria & archaea, protects against phage viruses.
Cas9 Protein: A nuclease that cuts viral DNA based on CRISPR RNA guidance.
Philippe Horvath (2005): Used CRISPR to protect yogurt cultures from phage attacks, improving shelf life.
Timeline: CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing tool developed in 2012 by Doudna & Charpentier

#15 Characteristics of Stem Cells - Mary
Obtaining Pluripotent Stem Cells w/o Embryos
- Use induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) to avoid embryonic stem cells.
- iPSCs are created by reprogramming adult cells to revert to a pluripotent state

Methods to Obtain Pluripotent Stem Cells from Adult Tissues
- Reprogram adult cells (e.g., skin) using factors like OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, c-MYC.
- These factors reset cell identity, making them pluripotent.

Culturing Stem Cells in the Lab
- Grow on supportive surfaces with nutrient-rich media.
- Regularly passage cells to prevent differentiation.
- Maintain strict control over temperature, pH, and sterility.

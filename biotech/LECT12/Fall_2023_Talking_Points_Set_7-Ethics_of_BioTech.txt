Dutch Elm Disease (DED) is a tree disease prevalent among various elm tree species. Its initial discovery traces back to Ohio in 1930. Strobel created a genetically modified strain of the bacteria Pseudomonas syringae, which, demonstrated the ability to protect elms from the fungus responsible for DED.  Dr. Strobel proceeded with experiment involving the genetically altered bacteria to combat the disease without FDA approval and was described as an act of "civil disobedience." The EPA reprimanded him, and two committees at Montana State University acknowledged his violation of federal rules. Dutch elm disease and emerald ash borer have caused substantial ecological and economic damage, resulting in the depletion of trees and alterations in urban and forest environments. It is imperative to continue research and implement effective management strategies to curb the proliferation and adverse effects of these destructive pests.

#8 Animal De-Extinction - Rudra
Limitations of Current Technology in Reviving Extinct Species:
Current technology faces limitations in de-extinction, including incomplete genetic information, ethical concerns, ecological challenges, and uncertainties in the survivability and adaptability of revived species.
Which animals are we able to bring back dinosaurs As of now, there is no technology or capability to bring back dinosaurs, as their extinction occurred millions of years ago, and complete genetic information for cloning is not available.
Which animals are we able to bring back mammoths: Scientists are exploring the possibility of bringing back mammoths through genetic engineering, particularly by using closely related elephants as surrogate mothers for hybrid mammoth-elephant embryos.
Which animals are we able to bring back dodo birds:  There are currently no known efforts or technologies to bring back extinct species like the dodo bird as the genetic material required for de-extinction is not available.
Which animals are we able to bring back plants species: Reviving extinct plant species is currently not feasible as it requires intact, viable seeds or genetic material, and the technology for de-extinction in plants is in early stages of development.

De-extinction could impact biodiversity conservation efforts by potentially restoring lost species, but ethical, ecological, and resource allocation challenges may divert attention from existing conservation priorities.

Ethical Sourcing of Biological Materials-shubham 
Who was Henrietta Lacks, and why is her story significant in the context of ethical sourcing of biological materials?
Henrietta Lacks was an African American woman from Roanoke, Virginia, who unknowingly contributed to a groundbreaking medical discovery when her cervical cancer cells were taken without her consent in 1951. These cells, later known as HeLa cells, became the first human cells to reproduce continuously in a laboratory, revolutionizing biological research and leading to significant advancements in medicine. 
Henrietta Lacks' story is significant : Henrietta Lacks' family never received any compensation or recognition for their contribution to science. This raised questions about the fairness of research practices and the need for mechanisms to ensure that research participants benefit from the use of their biological materials.Henrietta Lacks' story brought to light the need for researchers to respect the rights of their participants, including their privacy, confidentiality, and control over their biological materials.
How important is informed consent in the collection of human tissues for research? Can you provide an example where consent was not properly obtained?
Informed consent is of paramount importance in the collection of human tissues for research. It ensures that potential donors are adequately informed about the research's risks and benefits and have freely and voluntarily agreed to participate. This ethical principle safeguards individual autonomy, protects against potential harm, fosters trust in research, and adheres to regulations.

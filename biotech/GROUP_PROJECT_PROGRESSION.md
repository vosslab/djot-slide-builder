# Group project slide progression

Use this plan when preparing each week's group project guide. The teaching emphasis changes
as groups move from choosing an idea to explaining the science and completing the business plan.
Keep the instructor's wording, examples, humor, and meaningful color cues. Adjust dates,
visibility, and emphasis; preserve existing prose unless the instructor authorizes a change.

## Weekly teaching progression

This is a preparation guide. Select the material students need for the next decision, while
keeping later requirements available as reference. A reminder can recur without replaying the
whole guide. Do not add graded tasks or infer new deadlines from this table.

| Stage | Main teaching emphasis | Show or revisit | Keep brief or in reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduce the project | Move from individual ideas to one group company. | Overall milestones; group size; collaboration roles; vision-report purpose and example; presentation date and point preview. | Detailed presentation instructions, business-plan rubrics, and final submission logistics. |
| Form groups | Get the people working together and narrow the product. | Roles; communication and next meeting; one shared vision; product and biological mechanism; core presentation expectations and story; business-plan structure and major pitfalls. | Full rubrics, upload instructions, evaluation screenshots, and final reminders. |
| Choose the product | Commit to one company direction through the vision report. | Changes to the individual idea; specific science, market, competition, and milestones; once-per-group form reminder. Make the full presentation and plan rubrics available as reference by this stage. | Settled group-formation instructions. Discuss the rubric sections that help the groups' current decisions. |
| Develop and rehearse | Turn research into a connected scientific story. | Detailed presentation rubric and tips; shared speaking roles; timing; visuals; competition and milestones. Revisit plan sections and rubric weaknesses while there is time to improve them. | Vision-report instructions after submission; generic launch material. Show recording/upload instructions when relevant. |
| Present | Help the audience evaluate and the groups deliver. | Current presentation order; essential timing and speaking reminders; peer-evaluation instructions and current form access; next submission deadline. | Long outline lessons, group formation, and completed vision-report work. Keep full criteria available for reference. |
| Finish the business plan | Assemble, check, and submit one coherent document. | Required section names/order; full plan rubric; executive summary; competition and milestones; formatting after upload; contribution form after submission. | Completed presentation logistics. Use reference materials rather than scheduling a class that does not meet. |

The transition is gradual. Introduce the plan structure early so groups can write while preparing
their presentation. Bring detailed rubrics forward when useful; reserve final upload checks for
the submission stage. Existing advice to work together across sections remains relevant throughout.

## Preparing the next guide

1. Check the current milestone, what students have completed, and the next deadline.
2. Compare the previous current-year guide with the relevant original progression example.
3. Keep useful wording and visuals. Change the agenda, deadline emphasis, and visible groups
   deliberately; do not rewrite the project instructions just to make a new week's deck.
4. Review both include-level and slide-level `hidden` settings. Revealing a chapter does not
   reveal slides that remain individually hidden; hiding a chapter hides its whole group.
5. Remove completed form links and obsolete logistics from live coverage. Check every active
   form's title and destination, current group names/order, and assignment dates.
6. Build the editable ODP and classroom PDF. Compare source/hidden/PDF counts, verify clickable
   links and native text, and inspect the rendered pages before delivery.

Record dated build results and exceptions in the lecture's review file. Keep this file focused
on the teaching progression and its current semester application.

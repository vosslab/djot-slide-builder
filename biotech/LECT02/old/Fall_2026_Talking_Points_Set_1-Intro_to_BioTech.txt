#2 Bacteriocins 
Bacteriocins are natural protein based toxins or peptides made by bacteria to kill or stop the growth of other usually related bacterial strains”
How are bacteriocins produced
Bacteriocins are produced inside bacterial cells through a highly coordinated, ribosomal biosynthetic pathway. Unlike traditional antibiotics, which are assembled piece-by-piece via complex enzymes, bacteriocins are coded directly by the bacteria's DNA and built using the cell's standard protein-making machinery.
Production process breaks down in to phase 
Environmental Activation 
Transcription and Ribosomal Translation
Post-Translational Modification
Cleavage and Secretion
Concurrent Self-Immunity
One slide per student

How do bacteria use bacteriocins
Bacteria use bacteriocins as biological weapons to eliminate competing microbes and secure vital resources within their environment. Think of them as heat-seeking missiles designed to clear out rival strains while keeping the producer's immediate community safe.


what are the mechanisms of action for different bacteriocins

Bacteriocins are classified into different categories based on their structure and Chemical Modifications . While their end goal is always to neutralize competing bacteria, different classes employ distinct 

Two primary mechanisms are 1.Membrane permeabilization
                                                          2.Non-Membrane targeting

How Can Bacteriocins Be applied in medicine?
In medicine, bacteriocins are viewed as the next generation of precision therapeutics, offering a highly targeted way to fight diseases without the collateral damage caused by traditional pharmaceuticals.

Bacteriocins are being applied and studied across several major medical areas

1. Combating Antibiotic-Resistant "Superbugs "

2. Advanced Wound Healing and Skincare

3. Cancer Therapy (Oncolytic Peptides)

4. Live Biotherapeutic Products (Smart Probiotics )

#3 Overview of Model Organisms (Raad Hejazin) 
Core Purpose: Decode complex biological pathways and perform controlled, reproducible research.
Selection Criteria: Simple genetic structures, rapid life cycles, small size, and affordability.
Advantages: High tractability, ethical viability, and accelerated discovery cycles.
Limitations: Evolutionary divergence and translation gaps in human disease and therapeutic modeling.
Industrial Applications: Living factories for mass-producing medicines, biofuels, and materials.
Key Concepts: Phenotypic plasticity and conserved orthologs.

#5 Early Evolution of the FDA - Vishal 
Biologics Control Act of 1902:-
Specific Goal: The Biologics Control Act of 1902 aimed to regulate the safety and efficacy of biological products, such as vaccines, serums, and antitoxins, to ensure public health.

Health Risks Prompting Enactment:The enactment was largely prompted by public health crises, notably the deaths of children in the early 1900s from contaminated diphtheria antitoxin. This highlighted the dangers of unregulated biologics, leading to increased demand for federal oversight.

Impact on Federal Oversight: The Act marked a significant step in establishing federal authority over biologics, leading to the creation of a more structured regulatory framework. It empowered the government to assess the safety and efficacy of biologics, thus laying the groundwork for future regulations and the eventual establishment of the FDA.
Pure Food and Drug Act (1906)

Dr. Harvey W. Wiley:Dr. Harvey W. Wiley was a prominent chemist and a chief advocate for food safety reform. He led efforts to expose the dangers of food adulteration and the lack of regulation in food and drug manufacturing.
Influence on the 1906 Act:Wiley's research and advocacy were instrumental in shaping public awareness and legislative action, leading to the passage of the Pure Food and Drug Act. His work emphasized the need for accurate labeling and safety standards.

Changes in Federal Regulation:The 1906 Act prohibited the interstate commerce of adulterated or misbranded food and drugs. It established the foundation for federal regulatory oversight, requiring manufacturers to provide truthful information on product labels and enabling the government to inspect and enforce compliance. This Act significantly shifted the responsibility for consumer safety from the states to the federal government, enhancing public protection against unsafe products.

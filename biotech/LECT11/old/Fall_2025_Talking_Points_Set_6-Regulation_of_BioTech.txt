#1 The Biologics Control Act of 1902
                            Why the U.S. Began Regulating Vaccines!

⚠️ 1. Trigger Event 
1901 Antitoxin Contamination Disaster
Diphtheria antitoxin from “Jim the Horse” became contaminated with tetanus.
Several children died after receiving the serum.
Created national outrage → demanded federal oversight.

🧪 2. What the Act Did
Required federal licenses for all vaccine/serum manufacturers.
Mandated inspections of production facilities.
Required accurate labeling (purity, expiration, manufacturer info).
Allowed the government to suspend or revoke unsafe manufacturing licenses.

🏛️3. Impact & Legacy 
Enforced by the Hygienic Laboratory (future NIH).
First U.S. law requiring pre-approval of biologics before public use.
Inspired the 1906 Pure Food & Drug Act → foundation of modern FDA.
Basis of today’s drug and vaccine safety systems.

Authority to oversee genetically engineered organisms posing plant-pest risks.

Environmental risk assessment of the GM trait (growth, survival, spread).Comparison with non-GM plants to detect unusual pest-like behavior.Evaluation of gene flow risk to wild or related plants.Monitoring for unexpected interactions with insects, microbes, or soil organisms.Review of molecular characterization (inserted gene, stability, expression).Containment measures and field-site controls to prevent uncontrolled spread.

Requires federal agencies (like USDA-APHIS) to evaluate environmental impacts before approving GM crop field trials or deregulation.Ensures preparation of Environmental Assessments (EA) and, when needed, Environmental Impact Statements (EIS).Promotes public transparency and participation through comment periods.Helps identify potential ecological risks, including effects on wildlife, soil, water, and biodiversity.

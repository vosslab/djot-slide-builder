Talking Points Guide

Talking Points Guide
Talking points were assigned during last week’s class
Each student was assigned to two talking points.
Each talking point will have at least two students talking about it
If you share a talking point with another student:
listen to what they say
try to not repeat or duplicate what they said
90 seconds of content is not much content!
Contributing gets you more discussion points
If you have something interesting to say about someone else's talking you can score some extra points.
Asking questions of other students gets you discussion marks as well

Talking Points Guide
Edit and update your slide
Add several pictures and some text
It is best to have as few words as possible on your slide -- you do the talking not your slide
Be prepared to give a 60-90 second presentation about each of your assigned talking point
define the talking point, describe its relation to biotechnology, or provide a recent example of the talking point in biotechnology news
Try to be unique
Don’t just copy the book, use the internet for research. 
Provide your own perspective
Please put into your own words
It is okay if you read your notes, but put notes in your own words
No direct quoting of an article, wikipedia or the textbook
Minimize jargon

Tips for a Good Presentation
Remember you telling a story. Should have a beginning, a middle, and an end. 
Respect the time allotted for the sake of your audience. 
If you go over time, they are no longer focusing on the talk, but watching the clock to see when you are going to stop. 
Explain at the level of the class. 
Have a technical term, define it. If you don't quite understand it, no one will. 
Because we are all novices, you will need to have a long intro. 
But try to engage the audience. 
Do not read from your slides; use notecards, if you must.
No complete sentences on slides, use short phrases, break the content up. 
Try to have a picture on every slide, helps maintain attention of the audience 
If you have a movie, talk during the movie, do not use narrator
Be prepared for questions. 
Show ENTHUSIASM when you talk and make it exciting! 

Best Advice
Be Enthusiastic
Use More Pictures and Diagrams on your Slides
be a visual storyteller
Use Less Words on your Slides
put the words in your notes, not the slide
Keep Things to the Point
Be creative with your slide with look and content; go beyond the book

The first to FIle system depends on the time FIled, First to Invent requires that one can provide proof of invention and proof of filing for a patent. 
Prior art refers to public knowledge at a time which might pertain to a patent. The change to this in 2013 includes prior art around the world 
First to File, a patent is given to one who files for the patent first, which the US switched to in 2013 to align with international law. 
The first to invent system was used previously and a patent was given to those who could prove that they had invented the product and were working to receive a patent were given the patent. 
Prior art and Publication refers to any public knowledge at a time relevant to a patent filing. For instance if one wants to file a patent on something which was public knowledge, that patent will be invalidated.

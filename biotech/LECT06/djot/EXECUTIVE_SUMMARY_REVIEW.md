# Executive-summary evidence for Lecture 06D

The revised [business-plan deck](lect06d-business_plan.djot) uses recurring writing patterns
in 31 PDFs from `~/Documents/teaching/CLASS/biol_480-biotech/01-Sample_Executive_Summaries/`.
All summaries were read using extracted text and page-two OCR where embedded fonts produced
damaged text. Selected Aquazyme, Nifty Genes, and Virona pages were also inspected as images.
This is an instructional review of the provided excerpts, not a grade or a scientific
validation of those proposals. Company names identify examples; student names are omitted.

## Patterns that changed the guidance

| Evidence in the samples | Teaching response in D |
| --- | --- |
| EnteroDetect opens with a concrete testing problem. | Start with a specific problem and affected customer. |
| Aquazyme uses clear Problem, Background, Solution, and Market headings. | Give each paragraph a job; preserve useful organization while checking claims. |
| Virona lists successive development tests. | Define milestones as evidence for the next decision; do not endorse its safety or timing claims. |
| Nifty Genes names nif genes and nitrogenase, then jumps to fertilizer-independent crops. | Name the mechanism and the unresolved step connecting it to a working product. |
| Several organ-printing proposals move directly from a patient's cells to a usable replacement organ. | Ask which tissue, which demonstrated result, and what must work next. |
| Chronic, FluCream, PurePod, and other summaries state broad safety, approval, partnership, or funding claims. | Separate published findings, company proposals, and targets; require sources and accurate status. |
| BioLights dismisses competition while proposing many lighting applications. | Compare against the customer's current solution, including conventional lighting. |
| Aquazyme compares ocean-cleanup spending with a small engineering input cost. | Identify the payer and the complete cost of delivering the service. |
| Stem Send uses financial figures without a clear revenue/cost/profit relationship. | Show a fully invented arithmetic example with explicit assumptions. |
| Hardware, AI, natural extracts, and conventional materials appear among the proposals. | Explain the molecular or cellular biotechnology that establishes course fit. |
| Many excerpts spend most of their space on background and broad benefits. | Reserve space for the customer, alternatives, measurable next step, and uncertainty. |

The strongest transferable features are specificity, helpful headings, and a visible development
sequence. Their presence does not validate every scientific claim in the same sample.
The four-paragraph scaffold and worked financial exercise are new teaching aids, not new grading
rules. The arithmetic example uses invented numbers and a negative operating result so students
can see why revenue differs from profit.

#3 Chromatography and Protein Tagging - Rudra
First application by M. S. Tswett 1903.
Chromatography is the ability to separate molecules using various partitioning characteristics of molecule to remain in a stationary phase versus a mobile phase. Once a molecule is separated from the mixture, it can be isolated and quantified.

Principle of Chromatography - Chromatography is based on the principle where molecules in mixture applied onto the surface or into the solid, and fluid stationary phase (stable phase) is separating from each other while moving with the aid of a mobile phase.

Column Chromatography is a preparative technique used to purify compounds depending on their polarity or hydrophobicity.
How does the column you used help purify the protein?
Affinity Chromatography

This chromatography uses a molecule binding specifically to a protein, or a ligand. The ligand is cross-linked directly to a matrix. After your protein of interest binds to the ligand, this complex then stays immobilized inside the column. Whereas, the unbound proteins flow through the column.
One of the most commonly used tags is the polyhistidine tag, also known as His-Tag, which is a string of usually between six and nine histidine residues
Affinity tags are highly efficient tools for protein purification. They allow the purification of virtually any protein without any prior knowledge of its biochemical properties.
The Strep-tag II is a synthetic peptide consisting of eight amino acids
Tagging can be done via cloning into vectors or added using CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing to tag an endogenous protein.

How does codon usage influence protein folding and overproduction?
“Consequently, to optimize protein production, codon usage must be considered. Although it takes a lot of work, genes may be altered in DNA sequence so as to change many of the bases in the third position of redundant codons. This is done by artificially synthesizing the DNA sequence of the whole engineered gene. For a whole gene, individual segments of DNA that overlap are synthesized and then assembled as discussed in Chapter 4. #9 Toxic Effects and Inclusion Bodies - Jannela
What toxic effects can occur from protein overproduction in bacterial systems?
Protein overproduction in bacterial systems can lead to toxic effects, including the accumulation of misfolded proteins, the formation of inclusion bodies, energy depletion, ribosomal stalling, oxidative stress, protease activation, cell lysis, impaired growth, and reduced viability. These effects can disrupt cellular functions and even lead to cell death. To mitigate these issues, researchers optimize protein expression levels, regulate production with inducible promoters, use chaperone proteins, and choose suitable bacterial hosts and growth conditions.
What are inclusion bodies, and how can their formation be prevented?
Inclusion bodies are dense structures within cells, often linked to stress and disease, composed mainly of aggregated proteins. To prevent them, ensure proper protein folding, control expression levels, manage stress, maintain proteostasis, address viral infections, and consider genetic engineering when needed. These strategies vary by context and aim to minimize inclusion body formation. Chaperone proteins
Describe techniques for refolding proteins from inclusion bodies.
Another technique involves adding chaperone proteins to assist in the refolding process. 
What are chaperone proteins, and how do they assist in protein folding?
Chaperone proteins, also known as molecular chaperones, help in the proper folding of proteins and maintain protein homeostasis. They work by overseeing the folding process basically and holding the protein in an unfolded state until the sequence completes. Once the sequence is complete it will then allow the protein to continue the proper folding process.
How does codon usage influence protein folding and overproduction?
Codon usage influences support in optimizing protein production, modify gene sequences by altering codon usage, synthesize and assemble gene segments, and add rare tRNAs or change rare codons to enhance production.

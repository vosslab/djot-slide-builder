# *Rapid Reproduction*

@left

- Mutation rates are low in animals and plants
- The average is about one mutation in every 100,000 genes per generation
- Mutation rates are often lower in prokaryotes and higher in viruses
- Mutations accumulate quickly in prokaryotes and viruses because they have short generation times

# *Rates of DNA Evolution*

@body

- There are only 4 nucleotides, but there are 20 aa&#8230;smaller number of subunits means changes at nucleotide sites are more likely than at amino acid sites
- Again, nucleotide sites evolve at different rates&#8230; depending on the function and position in the genome
  - Synonymous substitution &#8211; doesn&#8217;t result in aa replacement (Third codon position)
  - Non-synonymous substitution &#8211; results in aa replacement&#8230; (First and second position.)
  - Pseudogenes &#8211; fastest evolving then fourfold degenerate site

=== layout: one-panel

# *Rate of Protein Evolution*

@body

- Fraction of sites that undergo a change in some designated interval of time&#8230;
  - Ex\: beta globin molecule between Mouse and Human is 1.23 aa replacement per aa site per billion years.
- Mouse and Human lineages have been separated for 80 million years&#8230;.
  - the last time shared a common ancestor
- 147 aa long Beta globin chain&#8230;
  - About 29 different aa changes total or about 8 aa change in first 50 aa..

# Homologous Genes

=== layout: one-panel

# *Homologous Genes*

@body

- Orthologous genes
  - genes that are duplicated as an accompaniment to speciation and that retain the same function.
- Paralogous genes
  - duplicated genes that take on new gene functions.

=== layout: one-panel

# *Origin of New Species*

@body

- New genes come from preexisting genes&#8230;raw material comes from duplications of the regions of the genome
- [*Orthologous genes*]{underline=true}\: Groups of genes or proteins from different organisms that have the same function are said to be orthologous
- [*Paralogous genes*]{underline=true}\: Two homologous sequences (e.g., sequences that share a common evolutionary ancestor) that diverged by gene duplication, as opposed to orthologs, which diverged by speciation

# Key Takeaways

@body

- A gene tree can differ from the species tree.
- Sequence regions can evolve at different rates.
- Molecular clocks connect divergence with time.
- Homologous genes can arise by speciation or duplication.

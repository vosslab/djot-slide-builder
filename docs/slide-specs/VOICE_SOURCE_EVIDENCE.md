capitalization in these originals. Normalize case when applying that convention while keeping
the instructor's wording.

## What the evidence supports

- Retain concise complete statements, meaningful fragments, and short labels according to purpose.
- Preserve emphasis such as "can and do," necessary qualifications, and full scientific subjects.
- Keep context and assumptions in problem statements, even when the setup takes more words.
- Keep conversational first-person explanations recognizable in announcements.
- Preserve useful repetition when a worked or visual sequence develops the same example.

The approximately 10-15-word review cue is the instructor's stated preference, not a threshold
calculated from this sample or an experimentally established readability limit. Use it to review
new or explicitly targeted prose for elaboration, with flexibility for long subjects and necessary
scientific context. The prompt-supplied trisomy example is a preference illustration, rather than
an observed original-slide quotation.

This first sample covers one Genetics lecture. Additional courses and instructor-cleaned decks
can corroborate a broader convention. Keep recommendations and recorded authoring rules labeled
as such while that evidence grows; use [AUTHORING_GUIDE.md](AUTHORING_GUIDE.md#authority-and-evidence)
for the authority hierarchy. Source quotations establish wording use, not a fresh validation of
all scientific claims or the old slides' formatting.
